Before you send any prompt, ask yourself

  1. Does it fit my role? A Fact-Checker asks for facts and numbers, an Aid Investigator asks about money and results, and a Myth-Buster tests what people believe. Prompts that match your role earn more marks.
  2. Will the answer give me something I can check? Ask for a number, a year or a named place. You can't check "health is improving", but you can check "life expectancy is 84 (2023)".
  3. Does it use something from my list? Ask about things you already know from class. If you know the real answer, you will spot it when the AI gets it wrong - and that earns you a mark.

S01 - What is Human Wellbeing

Key words

WordWhat it means
WellbeingHaving what you need for a good life - it is more than just money.
STEEPSorts wellbeing indicators into five groups: Social, Technological, Environmental, Economic, Political.
GDPGross Domestic Product. The most common way to compare countries by money, but it does not show everything.

Big ideas

  • Wellbeing is more than money. It also includes meeting basic needs, fairness, caring for the environment, freedom and safety.
  • Wellbeing means different things to different people. Some put safety first, some put family and friends first, and some put connection to land first.

Test your AI with...

  • "Give me one wellbeing indicator for [country] in each STEEP domain, with a figure and year for each."
  • "How might wellbeing look different for people in [a rural region] compared with [the capital]?"
Catch it: Does the AI only talk about money? You know wellbeing is bigger than GDP.
2

S02 - Mapping Wellbeing

Key words

WordWhat it means
Choropleth mapA map that uses lighter and darker shades of one colour to show a pattern.
PQEPattern, Quantify, Exception. Three steps for reading any map or data. Quantify means use numbers, and an exception is something that does not fit the pattern.
Spatial patternHow things are spread across a map - where they cluster, and where there are only a few.

Big ideas

  • Countries with a high HDI are grouped together (Western Europe, North America, East Asia, Australasia). Countries with a low HDI are grouped in Sub-Saharan Africa and in places affected by war.
  • An exception is often the most interesting thing on the map, so ask why it is there.

Test your AI with...

  • "Where does [country] sit on the world HDI map compared with its neighbours? Is it an exception to the pattern?"
  • "What is [country]'s life satisfaction score in the World Happiness Report, and how does it compare with its HDI?"
Catch it: Does the AI use real numbers to show its 'pattern' (the Q in PQE)?
3

S03 - Trends in Wealth

Key words

WordWhat it means
GDPThe value of everything produced and sold inside a country, no matter who produced it.
GNIThe money a country's own people and businesses earn, no matter where in the world they earn it.
TNCTransnational Corporation. A large company that operates in many countries but sends its profits back home.

Big ideas

  • GDP asks 'where did it happen?' GNI asks 'who earned it?'
  • Neither shows whether money is shared fairly, or costs like pollution and stress.

Test your AI with...

  • "What are [country]'s GDP per capita and GNI per capita, and why are they different?"
  • "Which transnational corporations operate in [country], and where do their profits go?"
  • "What does GDP miss about wellbeing in [country]?"
Catch it: Has the AI mixed up GDP and GNI? Many sources do.
4

S04 - Trends in Health

Key words

WordWhat it means
Life expectancyThe average number of years a person in a country is expected to live.
DisparityA clear gap between groups of people or places.
HDIHuman Development Index. One score that combines life expectancy, education and income.

Big ideas

  • Since 1960, people live longer almost everywhere, but life expectancy has not risen by the same amount everywhere.
  • A national average can hide big gaps inside a country, including gaps in Australia for Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander Peoples.

Test your AI with...

  • "What is life expectancy in [country], and how does it differ between regions or groups?"
  • "How has life expectancy in [country] changed since 1960, and why?"
  • "How does life expectancy for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples compare with the Australian average?"
Catch it: Does the AI give one national average and stop there? Ask what that average hides.
5

S05 - Trends in Education

Key words

WordWhat it means
Quantitative indicatorSomething you can measure with numbers, e.g. GNI, literacy rate, life expectancy.
Qualitative indicatorSomething that is hard to count and usually comes from surveying people, e.g. freedom of speech, feeling safe.
EmpowermentHaving the knowledge, resources and opportunities you need to improve your own life.

Big ideas

  • The HDI has three parts: 1. a long and healthy life, 2. knowledge (mean and expected years of schooling), and 3. standard of living (GNI per capita).
  • Some things that matter, like happiness or freedom, are harder to measure (Happy Planet Index, Bhutan's Gross National Happiness).

Test your AI with...

  • "What are [country]'s mean years of schooling and expected years of schooling, and what is its HDI?"
  • "What qualitative indicators, such as safety or freedom of speech, describe wellbeing in [country]?"
  • "How do girls' and boys' access to school compare in [country]?"
Catch it: Does the AI get the three parts of the HDI right?
6

S06 - Inequality in Wellbeing (Syria)

Key words

WordWhat it means
DisplacementBeing forced to leave home because of war. You might move to another part of your own country (an IDP) or cross into another country (a refugee).
Humanitarian assistanceHelp with basic needs during or after a disaster - food, water, shelter and health care.
Transitional governmentA temporary government put in place after a major change in who runs the country.
ReconstructionRebuilding roads, buildings, services and jobs after a war.

Big ideas

  • War can set a country's wellbeing back by years, damaging health, income and education all at once.
  • Wellbeing can vary INSIDE one country, not just between countries.
  • Aid helps, but it can't fix everything (e.g. when funding is cut).

Test your AI with...

  • "How has conflict or political change affected wellbeing in [country]?"
  • "How does wellbeing vary between regions of [country]?"
  • "What humanitarian assistance or aid is there in [country], and what are its limits?"
Catch it: Does the AI only say what aid did well? Ask what it couldn't do.
Check every answer Whatever you ask, check the AI's numbers against an approved source (see the Sources tab): UNDP Human Development Reports, World Bank, Our World in Data, UNESCO, WHO, UNICEF or your country's own statistics office.

Where to check facts, and how to show where you found them.

Referencing - keep it simple (Rubric: Use of evidence)
1

In your writing

After each fact, put who said it and the year in brackets, for example (World Bank, 2023). Do the same for the AI, for example (Microsoft, 2026).

2

At the end

Make ONE sources list of everything you used. Set out each entry like this: Who. (Year). Title. Link.
For example: World Bank. (2023). Life expectancy - Kenya. https://data.worldbank.org

3

Check

Every source in your writing must also be in your list, and the AI must be in both places. Small mistakes in how you set it out won't cost you marks.

4

On your annotations too New - added 30/10

Every handwritten note on your printed transcript needs its reference in brackets, for example (World Bank, 2023). Write it clearly - if I can't read it, I don't mark it.

Can I use Grok, Perplexity or a different AI?New

No. You can only use Microsoft Copilot with your school login (recommended), ChatGPT or Google Gemini. Everyone uses the same tools so the task is fair and I mark everyone the same way.
Already started in another AI? Start again in one of these three.
Want to see links? Copilot shows them, and checking those links is part of the task.

How do I get the top marks?

Go deep - writing more is not the same as doing better.
Check at least five AI claims against sources that have a name and a date. Mark 16 or more claims on your transcript, with notes that explain each mistake. Catch mistakes AND explain why they happened. Include First Nations perspectives. Explain what is good about an initiative AND what it can't do. Show what your own thinking added.
Use the C-level Model Submission to see how to set it out, and the rubric to see what turns it into an A.

How will you know what I checked when you mark it?

Your Source Showdown table and marked transcript show your working. If you say the AI was wrong, the real number and the source must be right there for me to check. If they aren't, I can't mark them, and you won't have done what the task asks.

What if the AI doesn't fall for my trap?

That's fine - you can still get full marks.
Write your prediction first. Then explain what the result tells you about when you can and can't trust this AI.

Can I ask the AI more questions than the prompts in the table?New - added 30/10

Yes. You can show me much more of your conversation if you ask other questions too.
Just remember - every question and every reply has to be copied into your clean copy for me as well. That can get tiring, so ask questions that matter. Plan them out first - it keeps your assessment more succinct.

How many traps do I need to do?New - added 30/10

Just one. Your trap is Prompt 5 - one question with a false fact in it. Write your prediction first. You can ask follow-up questions about it, but it is still one trap.

This seems too easy - what makes it hard?

Getting the AI to write is easy; proving what is true is the hard part. You need to find the real numbers, catch mistakes the AI states confidently, spot bias and explain why. That is what earns the marks.

Do I hand in the whole chat?

Yes - hand in the whole chat.
Mark at least 10 claims. KEEP YOUR CHAT HISTORY ON YOUR MACBOOK UNTIL MARKS ARE RETURNED. I can only mark what I have, so there is no reason to delete your chat history.

What is the clean copy, and do I really need it?New - added 30/10

Yes, I need it. Your clean copy is your My AI Transcript Template with every prompt and full reply pasted in, saved BEFORE you mark it.
Print that copy, annotate the printed pages in pen, then paste clear photos or scans of them into Appendix 3.
Upload two files: your My Submission Template and the clean copy.

Can I use tables and graphs?

Yes, please do. Give every table and graph a source and discuss it in your writing.

Does the transcript count towards the word limit?

No. Only Sections 1-5 count (800-1000 words). The cover page and appendices don't count.
Tip: start every prompt with "Answer in under 150 words." This keeps your transcript short enough to print and mark.

Can I use Kenya?

No. Kenya is the class example, but you can still choose SDG 3 with a different country.
