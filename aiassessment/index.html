1

S01 - What is Human Wellbeing

Key words

WordWhat it means
WellbeingHaving what you need for life to be good - more than just income or wealth.
STEEPSorts wellbeing indicators into five domains: Social, Technological, Environmental, Economic, Political.
GDPGross Domestic Product - the most common (but limited) economic measure countries are compared on.

Big ideas

  • Wellbeing is more than money - it also covers basic needs, equity, sustainability, freedom and safety.
  • Wellbeing looks different depending on who you ask (safety first, relationships, connection to land).

Test your AI with...

  • "Give me one wellbeing indicator for [country] in each STEEP domain, with a figure and year for each."
  • "How might wellbeing look different for people in [a rural region] compared with [the capital]?"
Catch it: Does the AI only talk about money? You know wellbeing is wider than GDP.
2

S02 - Mapping Wellbeing

Key words

WordWhat it means
Choropleth mapA map that uses different shades of the same colour to show a pattern in data.
PQEPattern, Quantify, Exception - the three-step method for analysing any map or data set.
Spatial patternHow something is distributed across space - where it clusters, where it's sparse.

Big ideas

  • High HDI countries cluster (Western Europe, North America, East Asia, Australasia); low HDI clusters in Sub-Saharan Africa and places affected by conflict.
  • An exception is often the most interesting thing on the map - ask why it exists.

Test your AI with...

  • "Where does [country] sit on the world HDI map compared with its neighbours? Is it an exception to the pattern?"
  • "What is [country]'s life satisfaction score in the World Happiness Report, and how does it compare with its HDI?"
Catch it: Is the AI's 'pattern' backed by real numbers (the Q in PQE)?
3

S03 - Trends in Wealth

Key words

WordWhat it means
GDPThe value of all goods and services produced within a country's borders, no matter who produced them.
GNIThe value of what a country's own residents and businesses earn, no matter where in the world they earned it.
TNCTransnational Corporation - a company that operates in many countries but sends profits back to its home country.

Big ideas

  • GDP asks 'where did it happen?' GNI asks 'who earned it?'
  • Neither measure shows how evenly wealth is shared, or costs like pollution and stress.

Test your AI with...

  • "What are [country]'s GDP per capita and GNI per capita, and why are they different?"
  • "Which transnational corporations operate in [country], and where do their profits go?"
  • "What does GDP miss about wellbeing in [country]?"
Catch it: Has the AI mixed up GDP and GNI? Many sources do.
4

S04 - Trends in Health

Key words

WordWhat it means
Life expectancyThe average number of years a person is expected to live in a given country.
DisparityA noticeable difference or gap between groups or places.
HDIHuman Development Index - a measure built from life expectancy, education and income combined.

Big ideas

  • Life expectancy has risen almost everywhere since 1960, but not evenly.
  • National averages can hide big gaps within a country - including in Australia for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples.

Test your AI with...

  • "What is life expectancy in [country], and how does it differ between regions or groups?"
  • "How has life expectancy in [country] changed since 1960, and why?"
  • "How does life expectancy for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples compare with the Australian average?"
Catch it: Does the AI give one national average and stop? Ask what that average hides.
5

S05 - Trends in Education

Key words

WordWhat it means
Quantitative indicatorA measure you can count with numbers, e.g. GNI, literacy rate, life expectancy.
Qualitative indicatorA measure that's harder to capture with numbers, from surveys not counts, e.g. freedom of speech, safety.
EmpowermentHaving the knowledge, resources and opportunity to improve your own life circumstances.

Big ideas

  • The HDI has three dimensions: long and healthy life, knowledge (mean and expected years of schooling) and standard of living (GNI per capita).
  • Some things that matter, like happiness or freedom, are harder to measure (Happy Planet Index, Bhutan's Gross National Happiness).

Test your AI with...

  • "What are [country]'s mean years of schooling and expected years of schooling, and what is its HDI?"
  • "What qualitative indicators, such as safety or freedom of speech, describe wellbeing in [country]?"
  • "How do girls' and boys' access to school compare in [country]?"
Catch it: Does the AI get the HDI's three parts right?
6

S06 - Inequality in Wellbeing (Syria)

Key words

WordWhat it means
DisplacementBeing forced to leave home due to conflict - within your own country (an IDP) or across a border (a refugee).
Humanitarian assistanceAid that meets basic needs - food, water, shelter, healthcare - during or after a crisis.
Transitional governmentA government put in place temporarily after major political change.
ReconstructionRebuilding infrastructure, services and the economy after conflict.

Big ideas

  • Conflict can set a country's wellbeing back years - on health, income and education at once.
  • Wellbeing varies WITHIN a country, not just between countries. Aid helps, but has limits (e.g. funding cuts).

Test your AI with...

  • "How has conflict or political change affected wellbeing in [country]?"
  • "How does wellbeing vary between regions of [country]?"
  • "What humanitarian assistance or aid is there in [country], and what are its limits?"
Catch it: Does the AI only say what aid achieved? Ask what it couldn't do.
Check every answer Whatever you ask, check the AI's figures against an approved source (see the Sources tab) - UNDP Human Development Reports, World Bank, Our World in Data, UNESCO, WHO, UNICEF or your country's own statistics office.

How it lifts your mark - Geographical concepts (5 marks)

The rubric words are exact. The example sentences show what each level can look like in Section 2.

Sound - 3 marks

Rubric: You mention First Nations wellbeing.

Example

"The AI did not say much about Aboriginal wellbeing in remote communities. Aboriginal people have a strong connection to Country, and this is important for their wellbeing."

Thorough - 4 marks

Rubric: You include First Nations wellbeing and Country with a specific example.

Example

"When I asked about health in remote Aboriginal communities (Prompt 3), the AI only listed government programs. It did not mention Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisations, which are run by local communities. AIHW data (Source, Year) shows ..."

Extensive - 5 marks

Example

"Missing from both of my bias answers were the voices of Aboriginal communities themselves. Neither answer mentioned community-controlled services or how caring for Country supports wellbeing. This matters because an answer that only shows what governments do makes communities look powerless, and could lead a reader to support the wrong solution."

Can I use Grok, Perplexity or a different AI?New

No - only Microsoft Copilot with your school login (recommended), ChatGPT or Google Gemini. Everyone uses the same tools so the task is fair and marked the same way.
Already started in another AI? Start again in one of these three.
Like seeing links? Copilot shows them - and checking them is part of the task.

How do we get the top marks?

Depth, not length.
Check at least five AI claims against named, dated sources; mark 16 or more claims on your transcript, with notes that explain each error; catch errors AND explain why they happened; include First Nations perspectives; judge an initiative's strengths AND limits; and show what your own thinking added.
Use the C-level Model Submission for the set-out, and the rubric to see what lifts it to an A.

How will you know when you mark it?

Your Source Showdown table and marked transcript show your working. If you say the AI was wrong, the real figure and source are right there for the marker to check. Otherwise, they can't be marked and have not met the requirements of the task.

What if the AI doesn't fall for my trap?

That's fine - full marks are still possible.
Write your prediction first, then explain what the result shows about when this AI can and can't be trusted.

This seems too easy - what makes it hard?

Getting the AI to write is easy. Proving what's true - finding the real figures, catching confident errors, spotting bias and explaining why - is the hard part, and it's what earns the marks.

Do I hand in the whole chat?

Yes - the whole chat, in full.
Mark at least 10 claims and keep your chat history on your MacBook until marks are returned. The marker can only work with what they have, and there is no reason for you to be deleting chat histories.

Can we use tables and graphs?

Yes - encouraged. Give every table and graph a source and refer to it in your writing.

Does the transcript count in the word limit?

No. Only Sections 1-5 count (800-1000 words). The cover page and appendices don't.
Tip: start every prompt with "Answer in under 150 words." so your transcript stays short enough to print and mark.

Can I use Kenya?

No - Kenya is the class example. You can still choose SDG 3 with a different country.
